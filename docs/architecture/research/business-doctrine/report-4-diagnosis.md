# DIAGNOSIS AND CONSTRAINT THINKING — Hormozi Research Report

## A. FRAMEWORKS

### A1. The Hormozi Intake (his literal on-camera opening protocol)

Observed verbatim across the live-audit episodes (Ep 809, Ep 894): every business owner opens with the same four-part statement, which Acquisition.com clearly trains into the room. This IS the intake form:

1. **"I sell [WHAT] to [WHO]"** — offer and avatar in one sentence (e.g., "We sell residential construction to middle-aged women who like to spend their husbands' money").
2. **"We do $[X] in revenue"** — current annual or monthly run rate.
3. **"I'd like to be at $[Y] by [when]"** — the goal, which he uses to test vehicle fit (a large-group gym cannot produce $50M; the goal exposes the mismatch).
4. **"What's stopping me is [Z]"** — the owner's self-diagnosis, which he records but does NOT trust.

**Then the numbers ladder, in this order (executable):**
1. What's your primary lead source? What's the split between sources?
2. What do you spend per month on ads / acquisition? How many new customers does that produce? → he computes CAC out loud ("so, 225-ish per patient").
3. What's your LTV? What's your price point? → computes LTV:CAC out loud ("so you're making 20-ish to 1. That's not bad.").
4. What are your margins?
5. What's your churn? ("So you keep 60% of your customers every year?" — he restates the number back inverted to confirm.)
6. How many leads a month? How many sales a month? What's your close rate (yours vs your salesperson's)?
7. **Capacity probe: "How many more customers can you take right now?" → "So what breaks when you double?"**

**Stop rules:**
- If the numbers contradict the self-diagnosis within ~5 questions, discard the self-diagnosis immediately and say so plainly ("40% is not your constraint, man").
- If nothing breaks when you double → the constraint is the owner (fear/mental barrier/focus), not the business ("Well, then why don't you double?").
- Stop drilling once ONE number is clearly out of benchmark (50% annual churn on a sticky product; 15 leads/month despite 20,000 content pieces; 30% closer vs 80% founder close rate). That number is the constraint. Prescribe against it only.

### A2. Why he discards the owner's self-diagnosis (the core doctrine)

His stated principle (Ep 894, near-verbatim): the things we love are rarely the things the business needs. **The constraint is typically NOT the thing you're good at** — the business grew because of your strength, so you keep proposing more of your strength. Product people ask for marketing hacks when marketing is fine; promoters ask for traffic hacks when the product sucks. Worked example: a man paid to fly to Vegas, and used his one question on the CLOSER sales framework. His close rate: 40%. "40% is not your constraint." He asked because he loves sales. Corollary: **owners solve comfortable problems; the constraint is the hard problem, and "if there's no hard part about the business you're in, that sucks" — the pot of gold sits behind the hard one.**

### A3. Constraint-identification rubric (signals per constraint)

One constraint at a time. The business is limited by exactly one of these; the numbers ladder reveals which:

| Constraint | Signals that indicate it | His words |
|---|---|---|
| **LEADS (demand)** | Few leads/month relative to content/outreach volume; capacity idle; owner is order-taking not choosing; business type is "usually demand constrained" (advice/wealth/professional services) | "Getting two sales a month is probably the big bottleneck" |
| **SALES/CONVERSION** | Leads exist but close rate low; big gap between founder close rate and team close rate (80% vs 30% = motion, not market); friction-heavy funnel | "I can almost guarantee you have a sales motion issue" |
| **DELIVERY/CAPACITY** | Full beds/full calendar/full trucks; phones "ringing off the hook" but can't service; hiring or comp is what breaks when you double | "What breaks when you double?" — whatever breaks first is the operating constraint |
| **RETENTION/CHURN** | Growth stalls despite good CAC ratios; local reputation burning; leaky bucket makes ad spend pointless | "Fix the hole in the boat... you learned how to market and sell, you just didn't learn how to profit" |
| **PROFIT-PER-UNIT (price/comp)** | At capacity, everyone happy, no money left; comp structure gives away margin (50% rev share while owner fronts all costs); underpricing ("about half of you are underpriced") | "Raise prices. You'll lose some and replace them with better ones and you'll make more money" |
| **META: FOCUS (owner)** | Two businesses/avatars/products; owner hours near zero on the winner; unmade decisions | "You have a focus issue. Put a star by that one." |

**The Seven Deadly Growth Sins (Ep 894) — his decision-impasse taxonomy.** Each is a choice between "hard today vs hard forever," and owners stay stuck by not deciding: (1) **Avatar** — serving 2-3 customer types; pick the one you serve best even if revenue dips. (2) **Data** — an important decision blocked on missing information; either put resources on capturing the data, or just decide: "if you can make the decision, fail, and go back and do the other path in less time than gathering the data, you make a failure in judgment by not deciding." (3) **Focus** — second business: "It's better to have one $2M business than two $1M businesses... your competitors who are beating you are only focused on one." (4) **Over-expansion** — location 2 opened before the talent existed; solution is the painful hard call, compressing "10 years of waffling pain into 6-12 months." (5) **Compensation** — underpay (can't recruit techs while phones ring) or overpay (50% rev share). (6) **Underpricing.** (7) **Single product** — hundreds of units/month with no back end: reframe customers as "highly qualified leads" whose purchases offset CAC for the missing main offer.

### A4. One-constraint execution doctrine

- **Everyone on the one constraint.** Fixing constraints in parallel takes longer than sequentially. "Could we improve this thing? Probably. Do we need to right now? No. It's not the constraint."
- **The Silent Sixth:** the standing priority underneath the one priority — keep selling, keep delivering. The proven engine never stops while the constraint gets attacked.
- **Bottleneck ping-pong:** fix CRO → call volume surges → sales becomes constraint → hire closers → marketing must refill capacity → success/delivery becomes constraint. Diagnosis is a loop, not a one-off; re-run the ladder after each fix.
- **More → Better → New**, in that order, as the prioritization spine: do MORE of what already works (lowest risk, "until volume is the constraint"); make it BETTER only when volume is maxed (weekly small tests; conversion-rate issues hide because the business prints money DESPITE the funnel); do NEW only when more and better are exhausted (highest risk; the default owner instinct and usually wrong). Operationalized as **70/20/10**: 70% of spend/effort on exact winners, 20% variations, 10% genuinely new tests.
- **Strategy definition he repeats:** "Strategy is how you allocate your limited resources against unlimited options." Filter for every idea: "Does this get me more customers? Does it make current customers worth more?" If neither, drop it. Leaving a workshop with 8 pages of notes and doing all of it = failure; the output is 1-3 moves.

### A5. Stage-based sequencing table

| Stage | Focus | Ignore |
|---|---|---|
| **$0-10k/mo** | Cash over perfection: sell before you build, collect up front. Warm outreach first (your phone contacts), then one Core-4 channel. Rule of 100: 100 primary actions/day. Founder does all sales. "You have a pulse and a credit card? You're for me" — take anyone, it's normal at this size. | Brand, logo, website polish, hiring, second offers, automation, "passive income" models |
| **$10k-50k/mo** | The Rule of One: ONE avatar, ONE product, ONE channel until ~$1M/yr. Master a puddle before the pond. Raise prices (half of everyone is underpriced). Fix the sales motion; document what you do (4Cs: camcorder → course → cadence → checklist). First hires replace the founder's lowest-value hours. Pre-sell launches hard. | New avatars, new products, new channels, personal brand, over-expansion to site #2 |
| **$100k-400k/mo (~$1-5M/yr)** | Retention and LTV become the multiplier ("the only thing that really matters long-term is that you keep people forever"). Profit is a discipline — force it, keep the line. One big bet per year on top of the Silent Sixth. Reinvest profit into the two capex items of service businesses: talent and brand. Weekly CRO/testing cadence; test changes in contained environments; expect a 20% short-term dip on any change. | Bootstrapping everything personally, parallel constraint-fixing, diversifying businesses |
| **$400k+/mo ($5M+/yr)** | Stop bootstrapping departments — buy subject-matter experts who've built the department before. CEO thinks like investor/allocator, not manager. Talent recruitment speed = scaling speed. "The theoretical max of a business is the sum of the knowledge on the team — buy more knowledge." | Founder as doer, company-wide untested changes, ego-driven revenue that blocks the avatar cut |

Skill-stacking order he prescribes for the person (Ep 601): (1) a thing to sell, (2) advertising/promotion, (3) sales/persuasion, (4) leadership and operations — matching Offers → Leads → sales → team.

## B. DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS & HEURISTICS

**Literal opening questions, in sequence** (compiled from Ep 809/894 transcripts):
1. "What do you sell, and to who?" (if muddy, he makes them re-state it)
2. "What do you do in revenue?" / "What do you want to be at?"
3. "What do you think is stopping you?"
4. "What's your primary lead source?" → "What's the split?"
5. "What's CAC? What's LTV?" — and when the owner can't answer directly: "What do you spend per month on ads? How many new customers do you get?"
6. "What are your margins?"
7. "What's your churn?" (restated back: "So 50% leave, 50% stay?")
8. "How many leads a month do you get? Good leads?"
9. "How many units do you sell a month? What's the price point?"
10. "What's your close rate?" (his vs the team's)
11. "How many more customers can you take right now?" → "So what breaks when you double?" → "Then why don't you double?"
12. "Are you doing this right now, month over month?" (tests whether the story matches behavior)
13. "What do you want to have happen? Do you want to sell this thing?" (goal-vehicle fit)
14. For any two named problems: "Those are two separate problems. Which one's the bigger problem?"

**Napkin-math patterns (his out-loud calculations, all sourced):**
- **CAC derivation:** $25k/mo ad spend ÷ 150 new patients = "225-ish per patient... so you're making 20-ish to 1. That's not bad." (Ratio floor ~3:1; at 20:1 the verdict is "spend more, not optimize more.")
- **LTV headroom:** $400/mo × ~20-month stick = "$8K LTV — you should be able to spend $500 to acquire a customer and not even sweat."
- **Churn compounding:** cut annual churn from 50% to 20% and "the business will just double on its own in two and a half years. You did nothing." Profit "significantly more than doubles."
- **The LTV multiplier case:** teeth-whitening chain — doubling lifetime revenue per customer → 2.4x revenue and 7.5x profit per location. "That's why I bought the company."
- **Guarantee math:** home-services owner at 80% close, no profit, scared to raise prices → raise 20%, guarantee "all my profit back if not on time and on budget"; on-time 8/10 times → give back 1-2 in 10, close way more, net way more. "This is a math problem."
- **Funnel-step math:** opt-in 35% → 50% = the business grows 42% with zero new traffic. Find the step where a 50% relative improvement is plausible (8%→12%, 30%→45%) and test weekly.
- **Owner-hours math:** "You work one hour a day. Imagine if you worked more than one hour a day... we 10x your time, you 5x the business."
- **Revenue-per-hour lens:** $350k/yr side brand at 1 hour/week = "$6,000 an hour on that side" → keep it, don't grow it, don't kill it; put all deliberate effort on the recurring side.
- **Workshop funnel constants:** lead gen → call → schedule → "remind the hell out of them" → expect 20-25% of leads to show → close a third of the room.
- **Opportunity sizing in seconds:** "You should be able to pencil out in five minutes: can we make more money from this?" (bathrooms: $10-14k each, 42% margin, 2-week cycle, 5/mo now, double with current crew → niche confirmed).
- **Market-size sanity check:** 2,000 church customers ÷ 300,000 US churches → penetration is tiny → "why don't we just double down and 20x this thing?"

## C. PRESCRIPTIONS (constraint × stage, sized for 2-10 person businesses)

**LEADS constraint**
- *$0-10k/mo:* warm outreach to the entire phone book; give free what others charge for; test lead sources cheaply — work 100-500 names from each source, double down on highest response; Rule of 100 daily.
- *$10-100k/mo:* swap the lead magnet, not the channel — "when we unlock an easy 5x it's usually something at the very front": from "book a call" to a valuable free thing (a workshop is his default for trust-heavy offers: advisors, legal, medical). Rotate the ad "wrapper" (6-week challenge / slim-by-Santa), never the sales process behind it.
- *$100k+/mo:* if intent-based ads are maxed, go interruption-based top-of-funnel; ad assembly machine: 6 angles × 5 hooks = 30 ads, then 70/20/10 weekly.

**SALES/CONVERSION constraint**
- *$0-10k/mo:* founder takes every call; trust-before-ask flips 1/100 into 10/100 ("solve one problem free for 20, then sell the next problem to them").
- *$10-100k/mo:* fix the motion before hiring: lead magnet → qualification → agenda for the call → pre-call nurture; "there's probably a double or triple sitting there." BAMFAM: book a meeting from a meeting, no lead without a scheduled next step. Add guarantee via the guarantee math. Kill low-ticket offers that cannibalize the core.
- *$100k+/mo:* close the founder-vs-team gap with recorded call review, weekly cash-collected competitions, reinforcement that is random/quick/public.

**DELIVERY/CAPACITY constraint**
- *$0-10k/mo:* usually fake at this size — it's a focus/hours constraint dressed up.
- *$10-100k/mo:* pay above market to hire the people who unlock revenue; commission-only where the sale funds the hire.
- *$100k+/mo:* simplify the model until it scales; stage-gate expansion with the no-person's checklist ("what would you need to see to feel comfortable doing the next one?").

**RETENTION/CHURN constraint**
- Any stage: fix the hole in the boat BEFORE scaling spend. Map the customer journey, set expectations correctly in marketing and sales ("the handshake between step one, two and three"), define activation points; light automation to cut operational drag. Payoff framing: churn 50→20% = business doubles by itself.

**PROFIT-PER-UNIT constraint**
- Any stage: raise prices ("you won't lose all your customers. You'll lose some, replace them with better ones, and make more money"); fix comp structure; match delivery intensity to price (12 meetings/yr billed below firms doing 1/yr = "doing 12x the work and billing less" — either tech-enabled volume or premium, "not a lot of room in between"); separate blended offers into two priced offers; monetize the customer base (single-product sin: sell existing buyers the missing main thing).

**FOCUS constraint (owner)**
- Any stage: pick one business, one avatar; the other becomes a cash-flow-only asset at fixed minimal hours or gets cut. Change the environment to change behavior. "The amount of work required to make something exceptional precludes you from doing multiple things."

## F. GAPS

- **The five-way constraint list (leads / conversion / delivery / retention / profit) is a faithful reconstruction, not a verbatim canonical list.** His recurring framing is the trio ("get more customers, make more profit per customer, keep them longer") plus capacity ("what breaks when you double") and the seven sins. Present the rubric as our operationalization of his practice.
- **The "makeover" videos where he visits a business could not be transcript-verified;** all worked examples are workshop/live-Q&A audits (same method, more reps).
- **The 10-stage Scaling Roadmap internals are gated;** stage table assembled from Rule-of-One content, workshop reviews, and transcript advice.
- **Rule of 100 details** verified only through secondary summaries of $100M Leads.
- Mild tensions for the synthesis to resolve: "take anyone with a pulse" (at $0-10k) vs avatar sin #1 — resolution: take the money, point all deliberate acquisition at one avatar. "More, better, new" vs prescribing a NEW lead magnet — the magnet swap is "better" (front-of-funnel fix on the existing channel), not "new"; make that boundary explicit so the doctrine doesn't self-contradict.
