# LEADS AND ACQUISITION — Hormozi Doctrine Research Report ($100M Leads territory)

## A. FRAMEWORKS

### A1. The Core Four Matrix (channel taxonomy + adoption order)
All advertising is one of four cells on two axes: **who you're talking to** (warm = people who gave you permission to contact you; cold = strangers) × **how** (1-to-1 private vs 1-to-many public).

| | Warm audience | Cold audience |
|---|---|---|
| **1-to-1** | 1. Warm outreach | 3. Cold outreach |
| **1-to-many** | 2. Post free content | 4. Paid ads |

Automation doesn't change the cell (a mailed 10,000-person list is still "1-to-1, done fast by a machine").

**Procedure (adoption order for a 2-10 person business):**
1. Everyone starts with warm outreach (free, reliable, works "as certain as the sun rises"). Do it until you have ~10 paying customers and testimonials.
2. Add content in parallel or next (free, compounds, warms every future channel).
3. Only then pick ONE of cold outreach (have time, no money) or paid ads (have money, no time).
4. **Stop rule:** master one channel before adding another. "Mastered" = you can predict cost-per-lead and lead→customer conversion within ~30%, a documented playbook exists a new hire could follow, and you could 10x volume without it breaking. Until then, do not add a channel.

**Why it works:** it collapses the infinite menu of marketing tactics into four mutually exclusive doors, so the diagnosis is never "which of 500 tactics" but "which of four cells, and are you doing enough of it."

### A2. Rule of 100 / Open to Goal (input-volume doctrine)
The minimum effective dose of advertising: **100 primary actions per day, for 100 days straight**, in your one chosen channel.
- Warm outreach: 100 reach-outs/day (call, text, DM, email)
- Content: 100 minutes/day making content, release at least one piece/day
- Cold outreach: 100 reach-outs/day (automate; expect low response rates)
- Paid ads: 100 minutes/day working on ads, or ~$100/day spend, 100 days straight

**Scoring/stop rules:** do not conclude "this channel doesn't work" until you have run it at Rule-of-100 volume for 100 days (a founder who sends 600 cold emails in 30 days has run 6% of the experiment). Graduate from Rule of 100 (effort-based) to **Open to Goal** (work until an outcome is hit, e.g. "5 booked calls today, however long it takes") once the channel is dialed in.

**Why it works:** most lead problems are volume problems wearing a strategy costume; a guaranteed floor of inputs removes the "is it me or the channel?" ambiguity.

### A3. Engaged-Lead Funnel Math
Definitions that gate everything else:
- **Lead** = anyone you can contact (a phone contact is a lead; a scraped list is leads).
- **Engaged lead** = a lead who has *shown interest* (opted in, replied, followed, booked). **Engaged leads are the true output of advertising** — count these, not raw leads and not impressions.
- Then: engaged leads → conversations → sales.

**Procedure (funnel audit):**
1. Count last 30 days at each stage: raw reach/attempts → engaged leads → conversations/appointments → sales.
2. Compute the three conversion rates between stages.
3. Fix the *worst* stage, not the top: few engaged leads from decent volume = weak lead magnet/offer; engaged leads but no conversations = follow-up speed/cadence broken; conversations but no sales = sales/offer problem (out of this domain, route to offers/sales doctrine).
4. **Heuristic:** a lead unworked for ~3 months has decayed back to cold. A 200-lead list you actually work beats a 5,000-lead CRM you don't.
5. **Speed-to-lead rule:** respond to new engaged leads in minutes, not hours; multi-day delay materially cuts conversion.

**Why it works:** it converts "I need more leads" (usually false) into a locatable bottleneck with one owner.

### A4. Lead Magnet Builder (7 steps, 3 types × 4 vehicles)
A lead magnet = **a complete solution to one narrow problem**, given free, that reveals the bigger problem your core offer solves. Try advertising the core offer first; if engagement is weak, build a magnet.

**7-step procedure:**
1. Pick a narrow, meaningful problem that sits one step before your core offer (home-seller example: "what's my house worth?" precedes "sell my house").
2. Pick a solution type (one of three below).
3. Pick a delivery vehicle: software/tool, information, service, or physical product (3 types × 4 vehicles = up to 12 magnets per problem; rotate them).
4. Test the name. The name/headline outweighs the content ("80 cents of your advertising dollar"); test headline, then image, then subheadline, in that order. Naming formula: specific outcome + specific timeframe + specific audience.
5. Make it easy to consume (under ~10 minutes; multiple formats; more hours/channels for services).
6. Make it *darn good* — good enough that you could charge for it. **Give away the secrets, sell the implementation:** the goal is to deliver more value than your core offer costs, before they buy.
7. Make the next step obvious: a CTA with (a) exactly what to do and (b) a reason to do it now.

**3 types:** (1) **Reveal a problem** — a diagnosis, best when the problem worsens with delay (website speed test, posture scan, termite inspection); (2) **Sample/trial** — brief full access to the core offer, best for recurring problems (free adjustment, free month, tasting); (3) **One step of a multi-step process** — best for complex problems (free template, first coat of sealant, free calculator that also reveals the work involved).

**Why it works:** people who pay with time now pay with money later; the free thing is the audition your whole brand gets judged on, and skimping is what actually loses customers.

### A5. Warm Outreach Engine (the start-here channel)
**10-step procedure:** (1) assemble your list from phone/email/social contacts; (2) pick the platform where you have the most contacts; (3) personalize every message; (4) send one by one, never mass-blast; (5) give value before asking; (6) ask "who do you know who…?" rather than pitching them directly; (7) make the easiest possible first offer (free/low-risk, in exchange for a testimonial); (8) start with your closest relationships; (9) start charging once you have results; (10) keep the list warm forever.

**ACA message pattern:** Acknowledge (something specific about them) → Compliment (genuine) → Ask (small, clear). Referral magic words: "Do you know anyone who…", "Who's the most X person you know?", "If you were me, who would you talk to?"

**Why it works:** it's the only channel with pre-existing trust, zero cost, and near-certain response, which is why it's the correct first hundred days of every new business.

### A6. Content Engine: Hook–Retain–Reward + Give-Until-They-Ask
Every content unit has three jobs:

| Stage | Window | Job | Tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | first ~3 sec / headline | stop the scroll, call out the right person | bold promise, provocative question, surprising fact, "most people do X, here's why that's wrong" |
| **Retain** | middle | keep them | lists, steps, stories, open loops ("later I'll show you…"), no fluff, high value-per-second |
| **Reward** | end | satisfy the reason they clicked | actionable takeaway, over-deliver vs the promise, frictionless next step |

**Cadence doctrine:** Give, give, give… ask (roughly 3:1 value to CTA), and the deeper rule: **give until they ask** — don't pitch; make the free material so good buyers come to you. Depth of value beats breadth of reach when small; go wide only after they ask.

**Repurposing waterfall (how one idea becomes many units):** one long-form piece (video/podcast/deep post) → cut into mid-length clips → splinter into 10-30 shorts/quotes/threads/carousels across platforms. Content compounds: the library, the followers, and the trust are assets that make every other channel cheaper (cold prospects who check you out see proof; ads to warm audiences convert better).

**Why it works:** attention is bought with the hook and repaid with the reward; audiences reward accounts that consistently pay off, and the platform algorithms follow the audience.

### A7. Cold Outreach Playbook (lists + personalization + big fast value + volume)
Three problems, three fixes:
1. **"No list":** build/buy one — scraping tools, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, directories, event lists. Aim for 1,000+ names before judging anything.
2. **"What do I say":** personalization (make cold feel warm: their name, something specific, genuine compliment) + **Big Fast Value** (lead with a giveaway so good they'd feel stupid saying no — not "good enough to charge for" but "stuff people actually pay for," delivered in the first 30 seconds).
3. **"No responses":** automate delivery, follow up multiple times on multiple channels, and when you finish the list, start over (circumstances change; wait a few months and re-run).

**Stop rules:** sub-~1% response = broken targeting or message, fix before adding volume; double-digit response = strong personalization, scale it. Follow up until they buy or say stop; most quit far too early.

**Why it works:** cold has no trust, so it substitutes volume + upfront proof-of-value for the trust warm channels start with.

### A8. Paid Ads Doctrine (Callout → Value → CTA; three phases; LTGP:CAC)
**Ad anatomy:** Callout (0-3s: label the person — "Gym owners", yes-question, if-then, ridiculous result) → Value (why care: make benefits huge, costs tiny) → CTA (exactly what to do + reason to do it now).

**Three phases:** (1) **Track money** — set up cost/click, cost/lead, lead→customer rate, cost/customer, LTGP *before* spending $1; (2) **Lose money** — expected and budgeted (plan to spend ~2x what one customer makes you in 30 days per test before judging); (3) **Print money** — reverse-budget from customer goals ("I want N customers → I need $X spend").

**Kill rules:** zero leads after ~1x 30-day-customer-profit of spend → kill. Leads but unprofitable → let it run to ~2x before killing. Profitable → scale slowly (10-20% budget steps), per More-Better-New.

**Gate to scale — LTGP:CAC** (lifetime *gross profit*, not revenue, over cost to acquire): under 3:1 you're struggling; 3:1 minimum viable; above 3:1 scale. If CAC is high vs industry, fix the ads; if CAC is normal but the ratio still fails, fix the business model (price, upsells, retention). **Client-financed acquisition:** engineer to recover acquisition cost within ~30 days (upfront pricing, order bumps, prepay) and ad spend becomes self-funding.

**Why it works:** ads are just money traded for attention at a measurable exchange rate; the phases stop owners from either quitting during the tuition period or scaling a loss.

### A9. More → Better → New (the scaling ladder)
When a channel works and you want more leads, in strict order:
1. **MORE:** do 10x the current volume of the working thing. Ask "what stops me from doing 10x?" (Usually: nothing but hours — that's a hiring cue.)
2. **BETTER:** when more breaks something, find the constraint stage in the funnel (A3), fix it, then do more again. Cycle more↔better repeatedly.
3. **NEW:** only after more+better are exhausted: new placements on the same platform → same content on a new platform → then, last, a second Core Four channel.

**Stop rule:** if you're "testing" 3-4 channels simultaneously and none is profitable after 6 months, you're spread thin — cut to one.

**Why it works:** new channels reset the learning curve to zero; doubling down on a proven channel compounds existing knowledge instead.

### A10. Referral Engine ("all customers must come from one customer")
Hormozi's forcing question: *you have one customer left and may never advertise again; all future customers must come from them.* That forces the two levers: make the product produce results worth talking about, and engineer the ask.

**Procedure:**
1. **Earn goodwill first** (referrals come from results): deliver the promise, smooth delivery, communicate constantly, fix problems fast, be fast, add unexpected extras. Also: set expectations slightly lower at the sale so delivery over-delivers.
2. **Ask at high-emotion moments** — 7 ask patterns: right after a big win; right at purchase (checkout-page intro request); as a negotiation chip ("discount for 3 intros"); referral events ("bring-a-mate week"); an ongoing program; unlockable bonuses (refer 3 → VIP); expiring gift cards they give to friends.
3. **Ask for an introduction, not a name:** a three-way text/email beats "got any numbers?"
4. **Program design checklist:** mutual benefit (give $X / get $X), one-click easy, unambiguous reward, tracked attribution, fast payout.
5. **Debug rules:** no referrals despite happy customers → the ask/mechanism is missing (most common); referrals dried up → product isn't delight-level, fix product before incentives; incentives ignored → reward too small or tracking distrusted.

**Why it works:** referred customers are pre-sold and pre-qualified by someone they trust, and (per the Level-4 milestone) a business whose product earns ~25%+ of new customers via word-of-mouth grows even with advertising turned off.

### A11. The Four Lead Getters (leverage layer)
Once *you* can get leads with one channel, recruit others to do it:

| Lead getter | Mechanism | Small-biz form |
|---|---|---|
| **Customers** | referral program (A10) | review + intro asks built into job completion |
| **Employees** | train them to run your proven channel | the #2 person does the outreach/posting you used to do |
| **Agencies** | pay specialists, extract the skill | "6-month learn-then-internalize" deal |
| **Affiliates/partners** | others with audiences promote for commission | the plumber↔sparky↔building-inspector referral pact; complementary local businesses |

**Delegation procedure (3Ds):** Document exactly how you do it as a checklist → Demonstrate it in front of them → Duplicate (they do it in front of you until they get your numbers). Never hire someone into a channel you haven't personally mastered; they'd have nothing to copy.

**Agency play (paraphrased):** open with "I want to learn how you do what you do; work with me 6 months, I'll pay extra for you to explain your decisions, then I train my team and we drop to cheap consulting — are you opposed?" Hire a good-enough agency to learn a platform's ropes, an elite one to learn mastery, and cancel when your in-house results beat theirs.

**Affiliate essentials:** give partners ready-made materials, pay fast and reliably, track attribution cleanly; wrong audience match kills programs regardless of commission.

**Why it works:** every layer multiplies the founder's proven playbook through other people's hours and audiences instead of the founder's calendar.

### A12. The Leads Roadmap (staging levels, sized down)
1. **Level 1:** your contacts know what you sell (warm outreach).
2. **Level 2:** consistent flow from ONE channel at max personal capacity (Rule of 100).
3. **Level 3:** someone else runs that channel (first lead-gen delegation, 3Ds).
4. **Level 4:** product good enough that ~25%+ of new business is referral/word-of-mouth.
5. **Level 5+:** multiple channels, multiple platforms, team-run (most 2-10 person businesses should aim to live at Levels 3-4 and treat 5 as optional).

**Why it works:** it stops small operators from cosplaying Level-5 marketing (four channels, an agency, a funnel stack) while skipping Level-1 basics (texting their own contacts).

## B. DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS & HEURISTICS (in the order he applies them)

1. **"How do you get customers today?"** If the answer isn't specific and confident, no channel is mastered; that alone is the diagnosis.
2. **"Do you have 10 paying customers yet?"** No → warm outreach, nothing else. Yes → continue.
3. **"Is the problem really leads?"** Check conversion first: if engaged leads convert to customers under ~5%, or LTGP:CAC < 3:1, it's an offer/model problem — more leads would just burn money. Fix offer before traffic.
4. **"Could you handle more work if it arrived tomorrow?"** If capacity-constrained or churn-heavy, fix delivery/retention first; don't pour leads into a leaky bucket.
5. **"How many advertising actions did you personally do yesterday?"** Almost always the answer is near zero. Volume heuristic: prescribe Rule of 100 before any strategy change. "You're not doing the wrong thing; you're doing too little of the right thing."
6. **"Which ONE of the Core Four are you committed to?"** If they name 3-4 channels and none is profitable, cut to one (channel-spread trap).
7. **"Have you asked your existing contacts/customers this month?"** Warm list + referral asks are the perennially skipped free channel.
8. **"What happens in the first 5 minutes after a lead comes in?"** Hours-later follow-up = fix speed-to-lead before buying more leads.
9. **"How many times do you follow up?"** Fewer than ~5 touches = fix cadence; follow up until they buy or opt out.
10. **"Would anyone pay for your free thing?"** If the lead magnet is fluff, that's why nobody engages: make it embarrassingly good and rename it (test the headline first).
11. **"What % of new customers come from referrals?"** Under ~25% with happy customers = there's no engineered ask; install one before spending on cold anything.
12. **Rules of thumb (his defaults):** warm before cold; depth before breadth; give first, always; more-better-new in that order; track everything or you can't improve it; keep rolling the die — the only losing move is stopping.

## C. PRESCRIPTIONS (situation → move, sized for 2-10 person businesses)

| Situation | Recommended move(s) |
|---|---|
| **Brand-new sparky/tradie, no pipeline** | Warm outreach 100/day through phone contacts + local Facebook groups using ACA + "who do you know who needs…"; free/cheap first jobs for reviews and photos; ask every finished job for a Google review + a three-way intro text. |
| **Cafe with foot traffic but flat sales** | Sample/trial magnets (Costco logic): tastings, "free coffee for a mate" expiring cards (customer-as-lead-getter); referral event weeks; content = daily short-form of the product being made (hook: the pour/the bake). Skip cold outreach entirely. |
| **Physio clinic, dependent on one referral source (GPs)** | Reveal-a-problem magnet: free posture/movement screen (problem worsens with delay); sample: free first adjustment with clear "permanent fix needs a plan" next step; formalize the GP/gym/pilates partner channel as affiliates with tracked, reciprocated referrals. |
| **Course/community creator, small audience, no sales** | Content at Rule-of-100 (100 min/day, ≥1 post/day) with hook-retain-reward; give away the secrets (the full method) and sell implementation (community, coaching, templates); one-step-of-many magnet (the first module free); give-give-give-ask cadence, don't pitch until they ask. |
| **Established trades/clinic, "word of mouth only", wants growth** | Don't jump to ads. Engineer the word of mouth first (A10: ask at job completion, mutual reward, three-way intros), then add ONE channel: content (before/after jobs) if time-rich, local ads if cash-rich, gated by LTGP:CAC ≥ 3:1. |
| **Owner posting content 3+ months, followers but no leads** | Check: are posts rewarding (usable value) or just present? Add a named lead magnet as the recurring CTA; put the magnet everywhere (bio, signature, post CTAs); if cash is needed now, layer warm/cold outreach on top since content compounds slowly. |
| **Getting leads, closing almost none** | Not a leads problem. Freeze acquisition spend, fix speed-to-lead (5-minute automated text+email), 5+ touch follow-up, then offer. More leads into a broken converter is waste. |
| **Ecommerce/local biz running ads at breakeven** | Track-money phase audit: know cost/lead, cost/customer, LTGP. If LTGP:CAC < 3:1, fix the model (bumps, bundles, prepay = client-financed acquisition) before touching targeting. Kill zero-lead ads at ~1x 30-day customer profit. |
| **Owner is the bottleneck; channel works but maxed** | More-Better-New says MORE first: 3Ds-train an employee to run the proven channel (document → demonstrate → duplicate); only consider an agency with the 6-month learn-then-internalize deal. |
| **Broker/consultant with a dead CRM of old leads** | Lead-hoarding fix: re-run the list warm-outreach style (circumstances change); 100/day ACA messages with a genuinely valuable giveaway; disqualify or work every name — a worked 200 beats a hoarded 5,000. |
| **"Should we try TikTok/a new platform?"** | Only if More and Better are exhausted on the current one. New order: new placements same platform → same content new platform → new Core Four channel, last. |

## F. GAPS

- **Exact ACA expansion:** sources agree on the acknowledge→compliment→ask pattern but the exact lettering may differ across book editions; treat the pattern, not the acronym, as canonical.
- **Specific response-rate and follow-up statistics:** "X% of sales happen after Y follow-ups" and precise cold-email benchmarks are sales-industry folklore, not verified Hormozi claims. Only directional stop rules kept (sub-1% = broken, double-digit = strong).
- **The ~25% referral threshold at Level 4:** appears in the third-party roadmap synthesis; could not verify the exact percentage against a primary source. Treat as directional.
- **Ad budget dollar tiers and kill thresholds:** the 1x/2x 30-day-customer-profit kill rules are consistent across sources, but absolute dollar figures vary by ticket size; sources themselves warn against fixed numbers.
- **Engaged lead → conversation → sale conversion benchmarks:** Hormozi gives the structure but not universal rates; the "<5% conversion = offer problem" heuristic comes from the founder-playbook synthesis, not verbatim book text.
- **Could not scrape:** Acquisition.com's own free-resources pages and YouTube transcripts (written sources were sufficient); no contradiction between sources found, only granularity differences.
