# MONEY MODELS AND CASH SEQUENCING (Hormozi doctrine, $100M Money Models + monetization mechanics)

## A. FRAMEWORKS

### A1. The Money Model (core definition + 3-stage build order)
A money model is a deliberate sequence of offers: what you offer, when you offer it, and how you offer it, to make as much money as fast as possible. Target: enough gross profit from one customer to acquire AND service at least two more customers in under 30 days.

Procedure (build in this order, never all at once):
1. **Stage 1: Get Cash** — build one attraction offer that reliably turns strangers into customers and covers acquisition cost.
2. **Stage 2: Get More Cash** — add an upsell offer (30-day profit well above CAC + cost of fulfilment), then add a downsell to rescue the nos.
3. **Stage 3: Get the Most Cash** — add a continuity offer to stack recurring revenue (one last sale inside the 30-day window; it is fine if the right moment lands after day 30).
4. Stop rule: perfect ONE offer at a time. Try it until it works reliably, then run it until automatic, THEN move to the next stage. Measure in quarters, not weeks.
5. Evolution sequence he states explicitly: get customers reliably → make them pay for themselves reliably → make them pay for other customers reliably → maximize long-term value → max out ad spend.
- **Why it works:** it removes cash as the growth bottleneck; each stage funds the next, so a bootstrapped business scales without investors. Warning he gives: "when your money model starts working, your business starts breaking" (operations become the new constraint).

### A2. Client-Financed Acquisition (CFA) — the golden equation
1. Compute CAC: everything spent to win one customer (ads, sales commissions, marketing labour).
2. Compute COGS/cost of fulfilment for that first customer.
3. Compute 30-day cash: net free cash collected from a new customer in their first 30 days (attraction + upsells + downsells + first continuity payment; ignore later LTV).
4. **Pass rule: 30-day gross profit > 2 × (CAC + COGS).** Example from his own video: CAC $100 + fulfilment $100 = $200; you must collect >$400 in 30 days; the $200 surplus funds the next customer. 2x is the minimum; 10x is better.
5. Why 30 days: it matches the interest-free credit card cycle, so acquisition can be floated on interest-free money indefinitely.
6. Failing state he describes (the death spiral): spend on ads → month ends underwater → cut ads → float with personal cash/loans → wait months for payback → fall behind → fail.
- **Why it works:** turns marketing from a cost centre into a self-funding loop ("negative acquisition cost"); the operator's wallet for leads becomes effectively bottomless relative to competitors.

### A3. The compounding growth identity
If you increase (a) profit per customer, (b) speed of acquisition, and (c) volume you can handle, growth multiplies: 2x each = 8x, 3x each = 27x.
- **Why it works:** the three levers are multiplicative, not additive, which is why fixing the money model beats fixing any single funnel step.

### A4. Attraction offer selection (Stage 1 menu, six mechanics)
Pick ONE, matched to the business. Free / $1 / discount are interchangeable points on one continuum of reducing upfront risk.
1. **Win Your Money Back** — pay upfront; hit defined results and/or actions within Z days and get it back (cash or store credit). See A5.
2. **Giveaways / raffles** — advertise a big prize; everyone who enters is a qualified lead; after picking the winner, offer everyone else a discounted "consolation" version. Key rule: raffle your most expensive service, not a generic prize, so every entrant self-identifies as an ideal buyer.
3. **Decoy offer** — advertise the free/DIY version; when they engage, present the premium alongside it (Gym Launch: free DIY plan vs $16k/16-week done-with-you; took him to $476k/month in 3 months).
4. **Buy X Get Y Free** — raises average order value; buyer spends more upfront to qualify for the free item (dog food: buy 4 months get 2 free).
5. **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** — discounted price + bonuses for acting now vs full price later; urgency without lying.
6. **Free With Consumption** — the thing is free contingent on use/consumption behaviour.
Profit comes from the behaviour the free thing triggers (the $127 "free month" storage-unit story: $0 lead → $47 lock + boxes + insurance + unit upgrade before move-in).
- **Why it works:** strangers cannot judge value from an ad, but they instantly understand price; a huge price-to-value gap is the only "deal" a stranger can verify.

### A5. Win Your Money Back design procedure
1. Set criteria of one of three types: results (lose Z kg / get Y customers), actions (attend all sessions, log daily), or both.
2. Criteria must be: easy to track (bonus if a device already tracks it), genuinely result-producing (copy what your best customers do), and include advertising the business (post progress, tag, review, refer).
3. Always have the next offer ready: pay winnings out as store credit applied to a bigger/longer purchase, spread over time ($600 credit → $50/month off a $200/month program for 12 months), never as three free months upfront.
4. Budget reality: his gym-network data says about 10% of customers claim the money back. If you cannot stomach refunds, skip this offer.
5. Refund rule (applies everywhere): anyone who asks gets refunded; reputation outvalues any single sale.
- **Why it works:** the customer bets on their own compliance; either they succeed (great testimonial, stays and buys more) or fund the program; motivation survives the early painful phase of any start-and-quit product.

### A6. Upsell doctrine (Stage 2a)
Principles first:
- Every solved problem reveals the next problem; the upsell sells that solution **at the point of greatest deprivation** (moment of need), not at the point of satisfaction.
- Five moments to sell: immediately ("fries with that"), at activation (first win), halfway point, at a milestone, last chance (before expiry/renewal).
- The front-end does not have to be profitable; the burger math: $0.25 profit on the burger → +fries +drink +supersize = $3.00, a 12x profit jump. "The thing you sell most isn't the thing you profit most on."
Four mechanics:
1. **Classic upsell** — "you can't have X without Y" (car wash → sealant; bike → helmet/lights; course → nutrition course). Offer the highest-margin upsell first.
2. **Menu upsell** — four stacked tactics: *unselling* (cross off what they don't need to build trust), *prescription upselling* (detailed, personalised instructions, not vague benefits), *A/B upsell* ("chocolate or vanilla?", never "do you want one?"), *card-on-file close* ("want to use the card we have on file?").
3. **Anchor upsell** — present the most expensive option first; if they balk, the mid option feels like a bargain.
4. **Rollover upsell** — credit some/all of previous purchases toward the next bigger commitment ("you've already spent $500; I'll credit it toward the annual").
Supporting plays: magnetic middle pricing (price the middle option to push people to the size you want; if everyone buys large, raise all prices), name your bundles (one ask, nine sales), sell guarantees/warranties/insurance for +5-50% instead of giving them away, BAMFAM (book a meeting from a meeting), "no-based yes" ("you don't want anything else, do you?" / "would you be against me waiving the setup fee if you commit to a year?" claims 80-90% take rates), exploit the hyper-buying cycle (new baby, new hobby, moving, wedding).
- **Why it works:** existing customers convert at far higher rates than strangers; timed at the moment the new problem appears, the customer upsells themselves.

### A7. Downsell ladder (Stage 2b)
Rules of downselling:
- A downsell is any offer made after a no. Never drop the price on the *same* thing (that is discounting; it destroys trust — the $5,000 car-insurance-to-$400 story). Change what they get or how they pay.
- No means no to THIS offer, not to everything. Downsells are trades: if you give something, get something. Personalise, don't pressure.
Three processes:
1. **Payment plan downsell** (7 steps, stop when they buy): (1) reward paying in full with a prepay discount rather than punishing with interest (present the with-interest price as the anchor, prepay as the saving); (2) offer third-party financing / credit card / layaway; (3) half now, half later timed to their payday; (4) check desire ("on a 1-10, do you still want this?" proceed only at 8+); (5) three even payments; (6) evenly spread payments across service duration; (7) free trial as last resort. Align charge dates with the customer's paydays to cut declines.
2. **Trial with penalty** — free trial contingent on meeting conditions (attend sessions, complete onboarding); card on file BEFORE explaining fees; penalties per infraction, not lump sum; mandatory check-ins double as upsell moments. Outperforms plain free trials because users actually consume and get value. If they refuse the card, they are not your customer.
3. **Feature downsell** — cut quantity, quality, support level, guarantee, or move to DIY, each with an explicit value attached ("without the money-back guarantee it's $400 less; which do you prefer?"). Name the bottom tier "the minimum" and the top tier aspirationally to nudge people back up.
Alternate between payment-plan and feature downsells within one sale; after two steps, re-confirm desire. Bartering variant: discount in exchange for reviews/referrals/testimonials.
- **Why it works:** "100 different offers for the same product" converts a large share of nos into yeses from leads you already paid for, raising 30-day cash with zero extra CAC.

### A8. Continuity conversion (Stage 3)
Continuity = ongoing value for ongoing payments until cancel. Trade-off he quantifies: $1,000 one-off to 100 people at 10% take = $10k now, $0 later; $50/month gets 40 of 100 to join and, at 20 months' retention, $2k now + $40k over time, plus 4x the base to upsell later.
Three mechanics:
1. **Continuity bonus** — join today, get a bonus worth more than the first payment (all past newsletters; free accelerator; free challenge). Advertise the bonus, not the membership. Bonuses must be things you already have/do (past content, onboarding), priced believably, related to the core offer. Status titles (silver/gold/diamond) work as zero-cost bonuses.
2. **Continuity discount** — free time now or later for joining today (first month free; stay 12 months, get month 13 free; lifetime rate lock: "pay $2.97 now and keep that rate for life").
3. **Waived fee** — month-to-month carries a setup fee; commit longer and the fee is waived; cancel inside the term and it is charged.
Sequencing plays: sell continuity LAST in the 30-day window, then immediately upsell a prepaid multi-month discount for more upfront cash; convert attraction offers to continuity via automatic renewal ("buy 6 months get 6 free" rolls into month-to-month); the free-challenge-into-membership swap (gym went from 34/100 challenge sign-ups converting 17 members, to 40/100 straight into membership plus 8 six-month prepays, by making the challenge free with membership).
Fit rule: only attach continuity where ongoing value genuinely exists; quarterly/annual billing dramatically cuts churn vs monthly.
- **Why it works:** you sell once and get paid repeatedly, and a bigger continuity base multiplies every future upsell; recurring revenue makes the business valuable and stable.

### A9. Unit-economics scoring rubric
1. **Gross margin floor:** service businesses should hold ≥80% gross margin ($100/month delivery cost → price at $500+).
2. **LTV:CAC by human involvement:** 3:1 if no humans in attract/convert/deliver (pure SaaS, ~5% of businesses); 6:1 with one human touchpoint (sales OR delivery); 9:1 (up to 12:1) with two human touchpoints (manual sales AND human delivery). Higher ratios buy the cushion for training lumpiness and hiring inefficiency when scaling.
3. **Payback period:** ≤30 days (see A2). For a small operator, cash velocity beats margin percentage: the constraint is money-in-hand this month to buy the next customer, not the ratio on paper.
4. **Retention benchmark:** 80%+ annual retention is the good-B2B bar; retention drives LTV drives every ratio above.
- **Why it works:** the ratios encode how much slack a model needs before scaling breaks it; small teams have the most human touchpoints, so they need the widest cushions.

### A10. Close-rate pricing diagnostic
Read your close rate on qualified sales conversations:
- 80%+ → underpriced 3-4x
- 60-80% → underpriced 2-3x
- 50-60% → underpriced 1.5-2x
- 40-50% → underpriced 1.25-1.5x
- 35-40% → priced about right (the target zone)
- <30% → do NOT cut price; fix the sales process or the avatar (targeting) first.
- **Why it works:** close rate is a live market price signal that costs nothing to measure; it turns "should I raise prices?" into arithmetic.

### A11. Pricing-by-stages and implementation discipline
1. Launch every new offer cheap to harvest early yeses and feedback.
2. As it gets reliable, raise price repeatedly; stop when the extra cash from remaining yeses no longer outweighs the added nos.
3. Simple scales, fancy fails: "less about 100 products, more about 100 ways to offer the one product" (personal training = 1/2/3/4 sessions per week = four offers from one product).
4. Fill money-model gaps with affiliate products (sell someone else's stuff for commission at any stage; massage therapist selling home massage tools; educator referring software).
5. Never plan-test prices in the moment out of fear; pre-plan price tests to a set number of people.
- **Why it works:** price discovery via volume-of-yes converges on maximum profit without market research, and affiliates add offers without operational load.

### A12. Cash pull-forward toolkit (payment structuring)
1. Reward prepayment with a discount (never punish plans with "interest": same math, better framing).
2. Charge setup fees / sell defined-length programs to concentrate cash into the first 30 days.
3. Bill on customers' paydays / when deposits land to cut declined payments.
4. Move monthly to quarterly or annual billing to cut churn and pull cash forward.
5. Use third-party financing so you get paid today while the customer pays over time.
6. Speed to lead: contact leads within 60 seconds; quick response roughly quadruples sales and directly lowers CAC.
- **Why it works:** the same revenue, resequenced earlier, is what makes the CFA inequality pass; for a 2-10 person business, timing IS the strategy.

## B. DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS & HEURISTICS

Questions he effectively asks of any business's monetization:
1. What does it cost you, all-in, to get one customer (ads + sales time + commissions)? Do you actually know this number?
2. How much cash do you collect from a new customer in their first 30 days, after fulfilment costs?
3. Does that 30-day gross profit exceed 2 × (CAC + fulfilment cost)? If not, which offer is missing: attraction, upsell, downsell, or continuity?
4. What do you sell SECOND? ("You barely have a business. You have a front end.") Businesses that add one upsell have "5x'd" from that alone.
5. What new problem does your first sale create, and are you selling the solution at the moment it appears?
6. What happens when a prospect says no? (If the answer is "nothing", you are leaving the downsell layer on the table.)
7. Do customers get ongoing value? Then why are they not making ongoing payments?
8. What's your close rate? (Map to the A10 table before touching price.)
9. What's your gross margin? (Service <80% → raise price or restructure delivery before chasing volume.)
10. How fast do you respond to a lead? (Over a minute is money burning.)
11. Could you offer someone else's product to fill a gap instead of building one?
12. Which single offer are you perfecting right now? (More than one at a time = red flag.)

Numeric rules of thumb (his actual thresholds):
- 30-day gross profit > 2×(CAC + COGS); minimum bar, 10x is the goal.
- One customer should fund the acquisition + servicing of ≥2 more within 30 days.
- LTV:CAC — 3:1 no humans / 6:1 one human touchpoint / 9:1-12:1 two touchpoints.
- Service gross margin ≥80%.
- Ideal close rate 35-40%; <30% = sales/avatar problem, not price.
- ~10% of Win Your Money Back customers successfully claim it (plan for it).
- Speed to lead: 60 seconds ≈ 4x sales.
- B2B annual retention benchmark: 80%+.
- Upsell take rate with a "no-based yes" framing: 80-90% claimed.
- Sales team utilization 70-75% (higher kills follow-up, lower breeds "commission breath").
- Rule of 100: 100 outreach/content actions a day for 100 days in one growth channel.
- Guarantees/warranties can be SOLD for +5-50% of price instead of bundled free (an art studio moved a free replacement policy to a 10% paid add-on; 30% attach, pure profit).

2. **Insta.page's "1-Page Summary" is unreliable**; only chapter-level content that cross-checks against the audiobook transcripts was used.
3. **Waived-fee continuity detail is thin** — definition and one framing example only.
4. **"Pick Your Price" upsell** appears in one breakdown but not in the audiobook's canonical four-upsell list or the Acquisition.com module index; treat as a peripheral tactic, not core taxonomy.
5. **The 80-90% no-based-yes take rate and some 2026-dated claims** come from third-party posts, not verbatim Hormozi; confidence medium.
6. **Business-type adaptation is example-driven, not a stated framework.** The C-table prescriptions are faithful transpositions, not his verbatim prescriptions.
7. Audiobook transcripts are auto-generated with noise; all numbers cross-checked across at least two sources where possible.
8. Australian consumer-law implications of "win your money back", trials with penalty, and waived fees were out of scope; he explicitly says to verify offer legality locally, and BOS surfaces targeting AU operators should carry that caveat.
