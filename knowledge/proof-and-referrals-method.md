# Proof & Referrals Method — turning results into word-of-mouth

The canonical knowledge for the floor's word-of-mouth skills: `build-my-proof`
(the transformation-story engine) and `set-up-referrals`. This is the method and
the templates; the skill bodies stay lean and reference this file.

**The core idea.** The strongest marketing a small business has is a customer who
got a result, saying so. A written 5-star review is weak proof; a **measured
before→after transformation in the customer's own voice** is the strongest proof
there is. This raises the **Belief** variable of the value equation
(`business-method.md` §6, variable 2: how sure the next buyer is it will work for
THEM) and feeds proof publishing (§10.5 tier 4). The whole method is: engineer a
result, capture it as a before→after, and turn it into proof and referrals at the
moment the customer is happiest.

**Strategy lives in `business-method.md` (one home, don't restate):** §6 (the
Belief and Arrival variables), §10.5 (review timing tier 2, proof publishing
tier 4, partners tier 5), §10.7 (the referral engine), §11.3 (activation — the
first win is where the baseline is set). This file is the how-to that executes
those.

**Shape guard (§15 — HARD).** Clinic/appointment and other regulated shapes must
NOT publish outcome testimonials, before/after claims, or result guarantees in
owned channels. For those shapes, redirect proof to service-level and process
proof (response time, turn-up-on-time, we-handle-everything, the experience),
never a clinical outcome. Check the shape before producing any testimonial.

## The transformation story (the play, across the whole engagement)

A story needs a before and an after. You cannot capture that at the end if you
didn't record the start, so the play runs in two moments.

### Kickoff — set up the win-story (at the start of the work)

1. **Name the target outcome.** What will be different for this customer because
   of the work, in measurable terms where possible? This is the Arrival (§6): the
   destination they picture, not the deliverable. ("Back to 20 leads a week,"
   not "6 coaching sessions.")
2. **Capture the baseline (the "before").** Record it now, while it's true:
   - Their starting numbers (the metric the outcome will move: revenue, leads,
     wait time, whatever fits).
   - Their situation and the problem, in **their own words** (a quote you can use
     later, captured with permission).
   - What they'd tried before that didn't work.
   - The goal they told you.
3. **Store it** so it survives to the end: keyless in a local file
   `proof/<client-slug>-baseline.md`, or on the customer's record once connected.

### Wrap — capture the win-story (when the result has landed)

1. **Pull the baseline.** If it's missing, reconstruct it from memory and say so
   (a reconstructed before is weaker but still usable).
2. **Capture the "after"** — the same metric, now; the change; what the customer
   says now.
3. **Compute the delta** — the before→after, with the hard numbers. That delta IS
   the proof.
4. **Produce the two assets** (below).

### Asset 1 — the written case study

Structure it `problem → solution → outcome`, with measurable `key_metrics` (the
same spine as a real case study asset). One-line summary at the top. Positive and
outcome-led. Use only real numbers the owner supplied and real customer words,
never invented quotes or metrics.

### Asset 2 — the video testimonial script

The single highest-value proof asset. A short script the customer reads on camera,
in their own voice, following the transformation arc:

> "I'm [name] from [company]. Before working with [owner/business], I was
> [the before / the problem in their words]. We [what we did — X, Y, Z]. Now
> [the after / the result, with the number]."

Rules: keep it ~60-90 seconds spoken; put the real number in it; it must sound
like them, not like an ad. Give the owner a few filming tips (good light, one take
is fine, look at the camera, say the number out loud). It is a **template with the
customer's real details** — never a fabricated quote.

## The quick review-ask (velocity path)

When the owner just wants more reviews fast (not a full story), the §10.5 tier-2
timing is what matters more than the words:

- **Who:** the person who did the work asks (not head office).
- **When:** at the moment of demonstrated satisfaction (job done, problem solved,
  the "thank you so much" moment).
- **How:** a direct link to the review page, one tap. Never "could you review us
  sometime."

The ask template:
> "Really glad we got that sorted for you! If you've got 30 seconds, a quick
> review helps other locals find someone they can trust: [direct link]. No
> worries if not."

Plus a reply pattern for every rating (reply to all of them — the reply is read by
the NEXT customer): thank the 5-stars specifically, respond to a low rating
calmly with a fix offered, never defensive.

**This review-ask script + timing has ONE home: here.** `get-found-online` flags
the review gap and points to `build-my-proof` to build it; it does not carry its
own copy.

## The referral engine (`set-up-referrals`, §10.7)

Two levers: results worth talking about (which the proof play creates), and an
engineered ask. The ask is where most owners leave money on the table.

- **Ask for an introduction, not names.** "Who do you know who's dealing with
  [the problem you just solved]? Happy to be introduced" beats "got any names?".
  A three-way text (you + customer + their contact) converts far better than a
  cold handoff.
- **Time it to the win.** The best moment is right after a result lands — which is
  exactly when `build-my-proof` captures a win, so the two hand off: capture the
  win, then ask for the introduction while they're delighted.
- **Make the reward mutual, one-click, tracked, fast-paid.** A reward for both
  the referrer and the new customer. Match the reward to the shape via
  `business-method.md` §15's referral & reward-inducement note (the canonical
  home): regulated shapes (finance/broking, clinic, insurance, legal) can be
  barred from cash or outcome-tied referral rewards, so use a service credit, a
  donation, or a thank-you there; non-regulated shapes may use cash/credit freely.
- **Keep it simple (§10.9).** Most small businesses live at referral level L1-L4;
  a plain ask + a tracking sheet is right. Do not build multi-tier affiliate
  machinery for a 2-person shop.

Deliverables: the introduction-ask scripts per moment/channel, the reward
structure recommendation, and a simple tracking sheet (who was asked, who they
introduced, status, reward paid).

## Output rule

Everything the customer or their audience reads (the case study, the video script,
the review-ask, the referral ask) follows the positive-only rule and uses no em
dashes (commas, colons, parentheses, or separate sentences). Frame everything as
the result the customer got and the win the next person can get. The owner-facing
coaching about *how* to run the play can name what's missing plainly (internal
voice); the customer-facing assets stay strictly outcome-led.
