# SEO Method — how the BOS helps a small business get found

The canonical knowledge for the keyless SEO work on the floor (the
`get-found-online` skill, and the search-lenses threaded into
`research-a-competitor` and `plan-my-content`). This is the method and the
checklists; the skill bodies stay lean and reference this file.

**What "getting found" actually means for a BOS-sized business.** For a local or
service business, being found is mostly two things: showing up when someone
nearby searches for what you do, and being the obvious pick once they land on
you. Both are winnable without a single paid tool. The heavy SEO machinery
(search volumes, backlink indexes, rank tracking) is a later, connected-tier
sharpener, not the starting point.

**Local strategy lives in one place: `business-method.md` §10.5 (the local
gravity stack).** This file does NOT restate it. When you prioritise fixes for a
locally-bought business (trades, clinics, hospitality, most services), the fix
order IS the §10.5 order, and the §10.5 hard gate applies: do not lead with
content or keyword work while answer-speed or a review-ask is missing. Read
§10.5 before ordering any local fix list.

**Keyless scope (HARD).** All keyless web reads follow
[`knowledge/research-method.md`](research-method.md): Firecrawl `scrape` /
`search` only, never `crawl` / `map` / `agent` / `extract` (those need a paid
key and are off-floor). Driver in the manifest, tools called in the skill body.
Read link and schema signals from the content of a page you already scraped;
never follow them into extra fetches.

## The on-page checklist (per page, read from one `scrape`)

Score each key page against this. Every item is visible in the scraped content,
so no crawl is needed.

| Element | What good looks like | Common miss |
|---|---|---|
| **Title tag** | Present, ~50-60 chars, names the service + the place ("Emergency Electrician, Geelong") | Missing, "Home", or just the business name |
| **Meta description** | One clear sentence, names the outcome + a reason to click | Missing, or auto-cut mid-sentence |
| **One H1** | Exactly one, says what the page is for in the visitor's words | No H1, or several competing H1s |
| **Heading structure** | H2/H3 break the page into scannable sections | A wall of text, or headings that say nothing |
| **Image alt text** | Describes the image (helps search + accessibility) | Empty alt, or "IMG_1234" |
| **Internal links** | Key pages link to each other (home ↔ services ↔ contact) | Orphan pages nothing links to |
| **Schema hints** | LocalBusiness / service markup signals present | None (a fine "nice to have" fix, rarely the top one) |
| **NAP (local)** | Name, address, phone visible and consistent | Phone buried, address only in an image |
| **One clear CTA** | An obvious next step (call, book, quote) above the fold | No ask, or five competing asks |

The on-page pass is rarely the *top* fix for a local business (the gravity stack
usually is), but it is cheap, fully in the owner's control, and a good source of
"do this today" wins.

## Reading a SERP keyless (the winnability read)

A `search` on a target term ("[service] [suburb]") tells you more than any volume
number for a small operator. Read the live top results for:

- **Intent** — what kind of page wins this term? A map pack, directories
  (hipages, Yelp), a competitor's service page, or informational articles? The
  winning page-type tells you what to make.
- **Winnability** — if the whole first page is national directories and big
  brands, that exact term is a slog; a more specific local variant is usually
  winnable. If local competitors with thin pages rank, the owner can beat them.
- **The gap** — what does the ranking page do that the owner's doesn't (a clear
  service page, reviews on the page, a price signal)? That gap is the fix.

This is directional, not a metric. It replaces "keyword volume" with "can I
actually win this, and what would it take" — which is the more useful question at
this scale. Real volumes/difficulty are a connected-tier upgrade.

## Topic selection for content (the search-intent lens)

When choosing what to publish (threaded into `plan-my-content`): prefer topics
that (a) match something people actually search, (b) the owner can speak to with
real authority, and (c) are realistically winnable per the SERP read above.
Aim content at the searches that sit closest to buying ("emergency electrician
[suburb]", "cost to rewire a house") before broad awareness topics.
`plan-my-content` reasons from the owner's terms or a spot-check they bring; it
never fetches a SERP itself (it is reasoning-only).

## What deepens when an SEO tool is connected

Keyless gets the owner a real audit and a fix list. These need paid data and
become available on the connected tier (surface them as outcomes, reactively,
never a cold pitch):

- **Real keyword volumes, CPC, difficulty** (vs the keyless winnability read).
- **Backlink profile** — referring domains, domain authority (no keyless slice).
- **Ongoing rank tracking** — positions monitored over time (keyless is a
  one-time spot-check).
- **AI-visibility at scale** — how often the brand appears in AI answers across
  many queries (keyless is a directional spot-check).

## Output rule

Everything the owner reads (the audit, the fix list, topic suggestions) follows
the positive-only rule and uses no em dashes (commas, colons, parentheses, or
separate sentences). Name a fix as a forward move and the result it unlocks,
never as a deficiency the owner "lacks". A rival's gap is fair as a sharp
observation; the owner's own position is always framed by what they can go win.
